# BlockWaveLab v2 Specification

## 1. Brand Identity
- Brand name: BlockWaveLab
- Brand role: Technical growth partner for Web3 teams
- Brand promise: Reliable delivery from implementation through operations
- Core principle: Engineering-first outcomes over campaign-first messaging

## 2. Positioning
- Positioning statement: AI Automation and DevOps Partner for Web3 Projects
- Market focus: Web3 teams that need production-grade infrastructure, automation, and managed operations
- Competitive posture: Practical execution partner with lifecycle ownership

## 3. Tagline
- Build. Automate. Operate. Grow.

## 4. Target Client Types
- Early-stage Web3 projects
- Growing Web3 companies
- Established/larger Web3 projects
- Enterprise-level clients where applicable
- Client size is never a restriction for qualification

## 5. Four Service Categories
- BUILD: DevOps and Cloud Infrastructure
- AUTOMATE: AI Agents and Workflow Automation
- OPERATE: Managed DevOps and AI Operations
- GROW: Growth, Content and Community

## 6. Services Under Each Category
### BUILD
- Cloud architecture and environment design
- CI/CD pipeline design and hardening
- Containerization and deployment workflows
- Infrastructure reliability baselines
- Security and access baseline setup

### AUTOMATE
- AI-assisted workflow design for delivery and operations
- Internal agent task orchestration
- Automation for reporting, triage, and routine operational flows
- Human-in-the-loop checkpoints for high-impact actions
- Prompt and policy versioning for controlled automation

### OPERATE
- Managed deployment and release operations
- Incident response coordination and runbook execution
- Performance and reliability operations
- Change-management and operational governance
- Ongoing AI operations support for deployed automations

### GROW
- Technical growth systems tied to product and operations readiness
- Content operations for authority and trust
- Community operations and enablement workflows
- Demand support integrated with delivery lifecycle
- Cross-functional feedback loops between growth and operations

## 7. 3-Day Trial
- Offer: Limited 3-day trial to validate fit and implementation readiness
- Entry point: Prospective client selects one or more service categories
- Trial output: Readiness findings, scope outline, and recommended implementation path

## 8. Trial Limitations
- Time-boxed to 3 calendar days
- Limited scope, no full production rollout in trial window
- Prioritized diagnostic and planning deliverables
- No long-term managed commitments finalized inside trial without formal transition
- Trial is designed to de-risk implementation, not replace implementation

## 9. One-Time Implementation
- Mandatory implementation phase before managed operation packages
- Includes technical setup for selected category or categories
- Includes baseline security, delivery flow, and operational handoff prerequisites

## 10. Production Deployment
- Formal deployment milestone after implementation sign-off
- Controlled rollout with environment checks and rollback readiness
- Deployment acceptance criteria must be documented before release

## 11. 60-Day Observation
- Starts immediately after production deployment
- Monitoring period for reliability, behavior, and operational quality
- Captures issues, bottlenecks, and optimization opportunities

## 12. Stabilization and Optimization
- Follow-up execution based on observation findings
- Prioritized fixes and optimization actions
- Objective: Reach predictable operation before handover or managed continuation

## 13. Documentation
- Deliverables include architecture, workflows, operational runbooks, and change logs
- Documentation must be complete enough for independent operation by client teams
- Documentation quality is a mandatory gate before handover

## 14. Handover
- Formal transition checkpoint after stabilization and documentation completion
- Includes ownership transfer, training context, and known limitations
- Handover can transition into optional managed service

## 15. Monthly Service
- Optional managed service after handover
- Month-to-month operational support and ongoing optimization
- Can include one or multiple service categories

## 16. Annual Service
- Optional managed service with annual commitment
- Includes the same category flexibility as monthly, with annual commercial terms
- Suitable for long-horizon operational continuity

## 17. Multi-Service Purchasing
- Clients can purchase one category, multiple categories, or add categories later
- Category composition is modular across BUILD, AUTOMATE, OPERATE, GROW
- Commercial and lifecycle flow must support phased expansion

## 18. Client Lifecycle
1. Trial qualification (3 days)
2. Service selection (single or multi-category)
3. One-time implementation
4. Production deployment
5. 60-day observation
6. Stabilization and optimization
7. Documentation completion
8. Handover
9. Optional monthly or annual managed service

## 19. Project Lifecycle
1. Discovery and scope framing
2. Technical design and implementation planning
3. Build and integration
4. Deployment readiness review
5. Production deployment
6. Observation and KPI tracking
7. Stabilization sprint(s)
8. Final documentation package
9. Handover and optional managed continuation

## 20. Pricing Architecture
- Pricing must map exactly to business model:
  - 3-day limited trial
  - One-time implementation
  - Optional monthly managed service
  - Optional annual managed service
  - Multi-category composition
  - Additional services purchasable later
  - Custom enterprise plans
- Pricing model must support category-level composition and upgrades without redefining categories

## 21. Organization Architecture
- Multi-tenant organization model
- Organization contains projects and category subscriptions/services
- Role-oriented access model for client teams and internal operators
- Separation of client-facing and operator-facing responsibilities

## 22. Client Dashboard Requirements
- View selected categories and lifecycle stage
- Track implementation and deployment milestones
- Track 60-day observation status and stabilization actions
- Access documentation and handover artifacts
- View active monthly or annual managed services
- View add-on and multi-service portfolio status
- View support requests and status timeline

## 23. AI Agent Architecture
- Agent-enabled automation layer scoped to approved operational tasks
- Tool and permission boundaries required per workflow
- Human approvals for high-risk operations
- Logging and auditability for all agent-driven actions
- Agents augment teams; they do not remove governance gates

## 24. DevOps Architecture
- Environment strategy with controlled deployment paths
- CI/CD standards for build, validation, and release
- Infrastructure and release workflows aligned to reliability requirements
- Rollback and operational continuity requirements included by default

## 25. Monitoring Architecture
- Unified monitoring for availability, performance, and workflow health
- Event and error visibility during observation and managed service phases
- Operational alerting tied to support and incident response workflows
- Monitoring outputs feed stabilization and optimization backlog

## 26. Support Architecture
- Structured support intake and prioritization
- SLA/SLO-aware response model for managed service contexts
- Incident lifecycle tracking from detection to closure
- Knowledge capture into runbooks and documentation

## 27. Billing Architecture
- Supports trial conversion, one-time implementation, and recurring plans
- Supports monthly and annual recurring structures
- Supports multi-category billing composition
- Supports upgrades and additional service purchases
- Supports enterprise/custom billing paths with explicit approval flow

## 28. SEO Direction
- Retire legacy crypto-marketing-agency positioning and metadata
- Reposition all SEO around AI automation and DevOps partnership for Web3
- Preserve reusable SEO infrastructure components where technically sound
- Replace all business-specific legacy service and keyword mapping content
- Keep blog/case-study technical pipelines if reusable, but rewrite business narrative

## 29. Security Requirements
- No exposed secrets in client-side runtime
- Access control model for dashboard and operations surfaces
- Validation and abuse protection on all forms and API entry points
- Audit logging for lifecycle, billing, and operational actions
- Secure deployment defaults and baseline security headers
- Security controls are mandatory gates before managed-service scale

## Governance Constraints for v2
- Do not alter the fixed business model
- Do not add extra categories beyond BUILD, AUTOMATE, OPERATE, GROW
- Do not introduce duplicate systems when existing infrastructure can be refactored
- Preserve reusable technical foundations where practical
- Remove only obsolete business-specific functionality with no v2 role
